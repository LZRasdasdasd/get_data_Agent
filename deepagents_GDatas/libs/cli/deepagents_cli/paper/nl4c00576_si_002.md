Experimental section
Synthesis of Fe2@NG DAC
1186 mg of Zn(NO3)2·6H2O and 30 mg of cetyltrimethylammonium bromide 
were dissolved in 40 mL of deionized water, forming solution A. 18.16 g of 
2-methylimidazole was dissolved in 280 mL of deionized water, forming solution B. 
The solution A and solution B are mixed, followed by stirring at 900 rpm for 20 min. 
The resulting mixture was subjected to centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 5 minutes, 
followed by overnight vacuum-drying at 80 ℃ to yield a white powder. Subsequently, 
2.1 mg of cyclopentadienyliron dicarbonyl dimer (Fe dimer) was dissolved in 150 mL 
of dimethyl formamide, followed by adding 150 mg of the aforementioned white 
powder. The mixture was stirred at room temperature under 800 rpm for 12 h. The 
solid sample formed was collected by centrifugation and then dried under vacuum at 
80 ℃ for 6 h. This solid sample (120 mg) was introduced into a solution of 300 ml 
Tris-HCl (0.05:0.015 M) and treated in an ultrasonic bath for 25 min. Afterwards, 45 
mg of dopamine hydrochloride was added, and the suspension was subjected to a 
further 10 min treatment in the ultrasonic bath, followed by stirring at room 
temperature for 6 h. After a subsequent centrifugal separation, a black powder was 
obtained and vacuum-dried at 80 ℃ for 6 hours. Finally, the black powder was 
annealed in an argon flow at 900 ℃ for 1 h, obtaining Fe2@NG DAC. 
Electrochemical experiments
Electrochemical measurements were tested on a CHI-660E electrochemical 
workstation in a three-electrode configuration including working electrode (catalyst 
coated on carbon cloth (CC, 1 × 1 cm2), reference electrode (Ag/AgCl), and counter 
electrode (graphite rod). The CC substrate was pretreated by soaking it in 0.5 M 
H2SO4 for 12 h, and then washed with deionized water several times and dried at 60 
oC for 24 h. To prepare working electrode, 200 μg catalyst and 5 μL of Nafion (5 wt%) 
were ultrasonically dispersed in 95 μL of ethyl alcohol to form a homogeneous ink. 
Then 50 μL of catalyst ink was loaded on a 1 × 1 cm2 CC substrate and dried under 
ambient condition. All potentials were referenced to a reversible hydrogen electrode 
(RHE) by E (V vs. RHE) = E (V vs. Ag/AgCl) + 0.198 V + 0.059 × pH. The N2ORR 
measurements were conducted in N2O-saturated 0.5 M Na2SO4 solution using a 
gas-tight H-type cell separated by Nafion 211 membrane. Prior to N2ORR test, the 
cathodic compartment was purged with N2O for at least 30 min to remove the residual

oxygen. During the chronoamperometry test, a flow of N2O (99.99%) with a rate of 
20 mL min−1 was continuously fed to the cathodic compartment. After each 
electrolysis for 1 h, the aqueous and gas products were detected by the colorimetric 
method and gas chromatography (GC, Shimadzu GC2010), respectively.1 
Flow cell measurements were carried out using a commercial flow cell 
electrolyser (101017, Gaoss Union Technology Co., LTD) comprising a gas chamber, 
cathode, and anode compartments (Fig. R3a). To prepare working electrode, catalyst 
inks, consisting of 3 mg of catalyst and 20 μL of Nafion (5 wt %) in 3 mL of ethyl 
alcohol, were sonicated for over 30 min and were drop-casted onto a gas diffusion 
electrode (GDE, Sigracet 39 BC) to obtain 0.1 mg cm−2 loading of catalyst. The side 
of the GDE that is loaded with catalyst was in contact with the cathode chamber, 
while the back side was in contact with the gas chamber. A proton exchange 
membrane (PEM, Nafion 211) measuring 35 × 15 mm2 was used to separate the 
cathode and anode chambers. Nickel mesh was used as the anode, and Ag/AgCl 
served as the reference electrode. Prior to N2ORR test, the cathodic compartment was 
purged with N2O for at least 30 min to remove residual oxygen. During the N2ORR 
test, NO was passed directly into the gas chamber at a rate of 20 mL min-1, and the 
electrolyte was continuously cycled through the cathode chamber at a rate of 25 mL 
min-1 under pump drive.
Calculations of NH3 yield rate and Faradaic efficiency
NH3 yield rate = (c × V) / (t × A)                  
(1)
FE = (4 × F ×c × V) / Q × 100%                  
(2)
where c (μg mL−1) is the measured NH3 concentration, V (mL) is the volume of 
electrolyte in the cathode chamber (35 mL), t (s) is the electrolysis time, A is the 
surface area of working electrodes (1 × 1 cm2), F (96500 C mol−1) is the Faraday 
constant, Q (C) is the total quantity of applied electricity.

Theoretical calculation section
All spin-polarized calculations were carried out by the generalized gradient 


 S-4
approximation (GGA) method with Perdewe-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional2 
based on density functional theory (DFT) as implemented by the Vienna ab-initio 
Simulation Package (VASP),3 in which the van der Waals (vdW) correction was 
described with the DFT+D3 method in Grimme’s scheme.4 The plane-wave basis set 
with a cut-off energy of 450 eV was employed. The convergence thresholds of the 
total energy and the Hellmann-Feynman force are 10-5 eV and 0.02 eV/Å, respectively. 
A 7  7 graphene supercell with a vacuum layer of ~18 Å was used to avoid the 
interactions between periodic images for all calculations. The Monkhorst-Pack 
meshes of 3  3  1 and 7  7  1 were adopted for structural optimization and the 
calculation of densities of states (DOS), respectively. The electron transfer was 
measured based on Bader analysis.5 
According to the computational hydrogen electrode (CHE) model,6 the free 
energy change (∆G) for each elementary reaction step can be calculated by following 
equation:
                         
∆G = ∆E + ∆EZPE − T∆S                     
(3)
where, ∆E is the reaction energy from DFT calculations, ∆EZPE and T∆S (T = 298.15 
K) are the contributions of the zero-point energy and entropy, respectively. EZPE and 
TS for the free molecules are taken from the NIST database,7 and those for the 
adsorbed species are obtained based on the calculated vibrational frequencies 
postprocessing by the VASPKIT code.8 And the corresponding values are listed in 
Table S3. The limiting potential (UL) is defined as −∆Gmax/e, where ∆Gmax is the free 
energy change of the potential-determining step (PDS). Here the UL is taken as 0 V 
when ∆Gmax value is negative. 
The formation energy (Ef) and binding energy (Eb) of the TM2@NG DACs can 
be calculated as follows: 
Ef = E(total) – 6(N) – 88(C) – 2(TM)                   
(4)
Eb = E(total) – E(NG) – 2E(TM)                   
(5)
where (TM), (N), and (C) are the chemical potential of the involved species, 
which is taken from the TM bulk, N2 molecules, and graphene, respectively; E(total), 
E(NG), and E(TM) are the total energies of DAC system, N-doped porous graphene 
substrate, and isolated TM atom, respectively. Note that the adopted DAC model 
consists of 88 C, 6 N, and 2 TM atoms.

 S-17
Fig. S13. (a) IC spectra of the electrolyte in the gas-tight H-cell for detecting the 
possible liquid N-contaminants after 30 min of N2O purging. (b) GC spectra of the 
gas-tight H-cell for detecting the possible gaseous N-contaminants after 30 min of 
N2O purging.
Since the N2O used in this work has a high purity (99.99%), no further purging was 
performed before electrochemical test. To verify this, we have used ion 
chromatography (IC) and gas chromatography (GC) to detect the possible 
liquid/gaseous N-contaminants (NH3, NO, NO3- and NO2-) in the electrolyte after 30 
min of N2O purging. As displayed in Fig. S13, neither liquid N-contaminants 
(NH4+/NO2-/NO3-, Fig. S13a) nor gaseous N-contaminant (NO, Fig. S13b) are 
detected, indicating that the NH3 synthesized during the electrolysis originates from 
the N2ORR process on Fe2@NG rather than from the reduction of other 
N-contaminants or NH3 impurities.

 S-20
Fig. S16. Characterizations of FeN4 SAC: (a) AC-HAADF-STEM image. (b) Fe 
K-edge EXAFS spectra and (d) WT contour map. (c) EXAFS fitting curve (inset is 
the fitting model).
The AC-HAADF-STEM image (Fig. S16a) shows the atomically dispersed Fe (bright 
dots) on NG matrix. As revealed in the Fe K-edge EXAFS spectra (Fig. S16b), FeN4 
SAC presents a prominent peak at 1.45 Å assigned to Fe-N bond, while the Fe-Fe 
bond is absent, further confirming the atomic dispersion of Fe atoms on carbon matrix. 
The same results are reveled in the WT contour map of FeN4 SAC (Fig. S16d). The 
EXAFS quantitative fitting result (Fig. S16c and Table S2) unveils that the Fe 
coordination number of FeN4 SAC is ∼4, suggesting that atomic Fe exists as the 
Fe1-N4 moieties (Fig. S16c, inset).

 S-22
Fig. S18. (a) Chronoamperometry test at various potentials on Fe2@NG in flow cell. 
(b) FEs of various products on Fe2@NG in flow cell. (c) FEs of various products on 
Fe2@NG in H cell. 
It is observed that FEN2 of Fe2@NG increases obviously in the flow cell (Fig. S18b) 
compared to that in the H-cell (Fig. S18c). This can be attributed to the higher N2O 
coverage on the catalyst surface (using GDE) in flow cell, which promotes the 
kinetics of N2 synthesis. Ko et al.’s work (J. Am. Chem. Soc., 2022, 144, 1258-1266.) 
demonstrated that the coupled N−N and single-N products are favored at high and low 
NO coverages, respectively, which are similar to our results. However, in flow cell, 
the primary product on Fe2@NG remains NH3 rather than N2 due to the 
thermodynamic favorability of NH3 synthesis over N2 synthesis on Fe2@NG.
