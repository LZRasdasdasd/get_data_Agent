**Text ****S****1**** *****Synthesis of ******Mn-MOF***

Mn-MOF was synthesized based on the previously reported method. [1,2] Typically, 0.247g manganese (II) chloride, tetrahydrate (MnCl2·4H2O) and 0.312g 1,3,5-benzenetricarboxylic acid were dissolved in 30 mL N, N-dimethylacetamide (DMF) solution, respectively. After complete dissolution, the solutions were ultrasonic for 5 min and mixed and stirred for 30 min. Then, the mixed solution was transferred to 100 ml Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclaves, underwent hydrothermal treatment at 140 ℃ for 12h. After the reaction, Mn-MOF was obtained by centrifugation, washed three times with DMF and methanol, and dried in a vacuum drying oven at 60 ℃.

**Text ****S****3**** *****Characterizations***

The crystal structure of the catalyst was determined by XRD (German Brooke D₈ ADVANCE) combined with JCPDS (Powder Diffraction Standard Federation) standard card, the scanning range was²θ = 5-90°, and the scanning speed was²°/min. Fourier infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) (American Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS20) was used to test the characteristic groups of the samples. The surface element composition and valence band energy were tested by X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (XPS, Thermo ScientificK-Alpha) and fitted by Adcantage. The adsorbed carbon (284.8eV) was used as the standard correction. Scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi Su-8010), transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-1230), atomic force microscope (AFM, Bruker Dimension Icon) was used to observe the surface morphology of the samples. The specific surface area and pore size distribution of the samples were characterized by Micromeritics APSP 2460 nitrogen adsorption and desorption analyzer. The content of Mn in the catalyst was determined by Inductively Coupled Plasma Emission Spectrometer (ICP-OES, Agilent 7800). Zeta potential of catalyst was measured by Zeta potential analyzer (DLS, Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS). UV-vis diffuse reflectance spectrometer (Shimadzu UV-3600I Plus, Japan) was used to analyze the light absorption range, and the UV-vis DRS calculation curve estimated the band-gap of the catalyst. Photoluminescence spectroscopy (Edinburgh FLS1000 spectrometer) was used to detect the recombination rate of photogenerated carriers, and the fluorescence lifetime of samples was calculated by time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy.

**Photoelectrochemical measurement**

The working electrode was prepared according to the following procedure: Catalyst (10 mg) was firstly dispersed in a mixture of ethanol (1 ml) and Nafion solution (20 L, DuPont d1202, 10 wt%) for 30 min. After which, the as-prepared suspension (100 L) was dripped on a clean FTO glass (1.0 ×1.0 cm²), and then it was dried in air at 200℃ for 2 h.

The setup of the as-prepared working electrode is as follows: A traditional three-electrode system that consisted of platinum as the counter electrode, Ag/AgCl as the reference electrode, and FTO with active material as the working electrode was used. A 0.5 M sodium sulfate solution (pH = 6.8) was used as the base solution. During the photocatalytic activity evaluation, a 300W xenon lamp with a 420 nm cutoff filter was used as the visible light source. The illumination duration was 20 seconds (on and off durations for 20 seconds each), and more than 4 cycles were conducted. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was recorded under an applied potential of -0.15 V *vs.* Ag/AgCl and frequency range (100 kHz ~ 0.1 Hz). The transient photocurrent densities were measured at an applied potential of 1.0 V *vs.* Ag/AgCl.
